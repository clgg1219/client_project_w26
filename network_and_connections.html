Guide to Networking

Why Network?

While your resume, portfolio, and skills are important aspects of job-hunting, connections are arguably just as important! Building strong relationships with those in your target industries and career area can help you learn about working in specific roles and companies, as well as gain valuable advice! Plus, networking helps build soft skills that are valuable in professional development, such as communication!

Who Should I Network With?

Networking can both be done with those who are well-established in their careers, or peers who are also starting out in their careers (they will eventually be other working professionals, just like how you will be)! Having a diverse network can help with gaining an equally diverse set of perspectives.

For those who are well established, here are some examples of who you can reach out to:

  • Working professionals currentlyin industry
  • Former employers (both for jobs and internships)
  • Professors
  • Current employer(s)
  • Alumni

If you want to network with peers, the following groups would work well:

  • Friends
  • Classmates
  • Older students (juniors, seniors, or second-year master's students)

How to Network?

Networking can be done in a variety of ways! It can be done online, in-person, or even on phone! It can be done through university, or non-university resources, through conventional and unusual methods- the possibilities are endless! Resources that are online will be linked below.


Here are some examples of online resources (specifically career-oriented):

LinkedIn Handshake University Career Alumni Network (UCAN)

Here are some more informal ideas for networking opportunities:

  • Online social media groups (such as Slack)
  • Class or friend group chats
  • Local career-related meetups
  • Any social event!
